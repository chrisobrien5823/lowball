# Surface brief: the daily game (index.html)

Scope: intro, question, reveal and results screens of the daily game. Visitor mode: Experience (the player is inside the game), with Operate rules for the answer input and timer. Build path: code-led (no image generation; the guides-only install skips concept-seed.mjs, so directions were derived by hand).

Memorable moment: the pen creeping toward the red limit line and stopping just short of it, or breaking through. The answer stays hidden until it stops.

History:
- Road signage (replaced 2026-10-24), then a chart-recorder instrument world with an enamel housing, bezel and amber LCD (replaced 2026-10-25).
- 2026-10-25: the content moved from countries to MLB, and the owner asked for no emoji and a more professional look overall. The recorder world was judged too skeuomorphic and costume-like for baseball stats, so it was retired. The earlier files are in `archive/design-recorder-2026-10-25/` and `archive/countries-2026-10-25/`.
- Hand of directions considered for the re-skin: (1) quiet stat sheet, a printed-page look with white cards and one red limit line; (2) dark broadcast graphics, a scorebug and navy glass; (3) keep the recorder and only mute it. The first was chosen as the least costume and closest to how baseball statistics are actually read (Baseball-Reference and newspaper box scores).

Unresolved: final name and domain.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A question is a line on a stat chart. The pen is the value you named, on a real axis, and the red limit line is the one thing on the page that matters. The chart refuses the trading terminal and the casino.

OWN-WORLD: A baseball stat sheet. Warm off-white ground, white cards with hairline rules, navy ink, baseball-stitch red used only for the limit, the bust and the trip zone. Archivo in weights 400 to 700 with tabular numerals. A dark theme keeps the same structure on a deep navy ground. No gradients, no shadows beyond the dialog, no ornament.

STORY: Read the limit card, type a player or team under the countdown, watch the pen move gradually toward the line, then read the verdict. Share five scores as plain text.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Desktop fills the window: a left rail (progress, limit card, prompt and source, answer) beside one tall chart card with the countdown in its header line. Phones stack the same parts.

FORM: Editorial stat sheet, chosen over the dark broadcast direction and the muted recorder.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, and DESIGN.md written from the built result.
